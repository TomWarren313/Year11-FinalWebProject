Problematic Social Media Use

Problematic social media use occurs when someone struggles to control their social media habits, even when they begin negatively affecting their everyday life. This can include spending excessive amounts of time online, neglecting schoolwork, losing sleep and prioritising social media over real-life relationships. People experiencing problematic use may constantly check notifications or feel uncomfortable when they cannot access their accounts. It is not simply about how many hours someone spends online, but whether their usage becomes difficult to manage and interferes with their wellbeing.

Social media platforms use algorithms to personalise the content shown on each user's feed. These algorithms analyse information such as likes, comments, searches, shares and viewing habits to predict what users are interested in. They then recommend similar posts, images and videos that are likely to capture their attention. Features such as endless scrolling, automatic video playback and personalised recommendations encourage users to remain on the platform for longer, making it harder to manage their social media use.

Are We Scrolling Our Lives Away?

My name is Tom, and I am a Year 11 student at Sacred Heart College in Auckland. I enjoy technology and computer science, and I hope to become a software engineer in the future. I like learning how things work, solving problems, and working on projects where I can put my ideas into practice. Outside of class, I am involved in robotics club, table tennis, and Young Vinnies. I enjoy being part of different activities at school because they give me opportunities to learn new things, meet people, and develop skills outside of my normal classes. Through Young Vinnies, I have been involved in community service and helping others, which is something I value. I was also fortunate enough to receive the Catholic Caring Award for my involvement in Young Vinnies. I would describe myself as someone who is curious and enjoys learning about things that interest me. I like a challenge, especially when it involves figuring something out for myself, and I am always looking for ways to improve my skills. I also value being able to work well with others and contribute to the community around me.
